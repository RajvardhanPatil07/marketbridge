# Competitive stack research: MarketBridge evidence firewall

**Decision date:** 2026-09-08
**Audience:** MarketBridge hackathon team
**Scope:** reusable components for a real-time mark-integrity and evidence firewall for 24/7 US-stock perpetuals. Sources are limited to official documentation, official repositories/package pages, and first-party terms.

## Executive decision

Do **not** integrate `vishal-tiwari9/hyperliquid-integration` as a dependency. It adds a second execution-oriented stack but does not strengthen MarketBridge's differentiator: defensible, replayable evidence for whether a perp mark is safe. Keep MarketBridge's FastAPI/Next.js architecture and its deterministic engine, source/venue independence checks, conservative fallback, audit log, and incident replay.

The strongest incremental stack is:

1. **Hyperliquid's official Python SDK/API, read-only and pinned**, for HIP-3 venue evidence.
2. **Alpaca SIP as the immediate consolidated US-equity source**, plus **Databento US Equities Mini as the best independent second live/historical source** if its subscription and data-use terms fit the demo. A free IEX-only feed is useful for development, not sufficient evidence by itself.
3. **DuckDB + partitioned Parquet** for durable evidence snapshots, fast replay, and policy-version comparison without operating a database server.
4. **River ADWIN/Page-Hinkley only as a shadow drift signal**; **ruptures** for offline incident segmentation and **MAPIE** only if MarketBridge starts publishing calibrated prediction intervals. Deterministic controls must remain authoritative.
5. **Prometheus metrics now**; add **OpenTelemetry traces** when a trace backend is available. Do not add NATS, Redis Streams, or a server TSDB for the hackathon unless the application is actually split into multiple independently deployed consumers.

The main commercial risk is market-data licensing, not SDK licensing. Open-source clients do not grant rights to redistribute their underlying equity data. Alpaca explicitly says its API data cannot be redistributed; Massive's individual terms prohibit business use, public redistribution, and many derived works. A public demo should expose MarketBridge's **decision, confidence, reason codes, latency, and carefully limited evidence metadata**, not rebroadcast raw SIP quotes, until the provider approves the use.

**Evidence topology recommendation.** During US regular hours, require agreement between a consolidated source (Alpaca SIP) and a separately operated vendor/feed blend (Databento EQUS.MINI), plus compare with the Hyperliquid venue mark/oracle. Outside regular hours, identify the session and source class rather than weakening thresholds invisibly: `regular_sip`, `extended`, `overnight_ats`, or `estimated_close_anchor`. A source, a venue, and a provider are different independence dimensions; record all three.

HIP-3 itself makes MarketBridge strategically relevant: deployers are responsible for oracle definition and operation; `setOracle` expects updates roughly every three seconds, clamps mark movement, and uses external perp prices to limit sudden deviations. Validator review can be triggered after very large external-price movement. [HIP-3 responsibilities](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hyperliquid-improvement-proposals-hips/hip-3-builder-deployed-perpetuals), [`setOracle` semantics](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/hip-3-deployer-actions). MarketBridge should therefore remain an **independent observer/firewall**, not become the component that signs or submits oracle updates during the hackathon.

## 4. Time-series storage, replay, and audit

| Candidate | Exact capability and maturity | License / risks | MarketBridge fit and verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DuckDB + partitioned Parquet** | Embedded SQL analytics; directly reads/writes Parquet with projection/filter pushdown, metadata inspection, globs and HTTPS/object-store access. DuckDB is MIT, highly active, and its 1.5 line received multiple 2026 patch releases. [Parquet support](https://duckdb.org/docs/stable/data/parquet/overview), [releases](https://github.com/duckdb/duckdb/releases) | DuckDB is analytical, not the multi-writer ingestion authority. Parquet files need atomic rotation, schema/version metadata and an external integrity scheme; DuckDB's native file format can have version compatibility constraints. | **USE now.** Keep JSONL as the immediate append/audit path, rotate canonical events into date/symbol/source-partitioned Parquet, and use DuckDB for incident replay, as-of joins, calibration reports and policy A/B comparison. Store source event time, receive time, sequence, raw hash, normalized fields, policy version, output and reason codes. |
| **QuestDB** | High-throughput time-series SQL with designated timestamps, partitions, out-of-order ingestion/merge, deduplication, ASOF joins and ILP/QWP. | **TimescaleDB** | PostgreSQL extension with hypertables/time-series analytics and a large active ecosystem; the active 2.29 line was packaged in August 2026. [repository/releases](https://github.com/timescale/timescaledb/releases) | Operational weight of PostgreSQL plus extension/version management. Licensing is split: Apache edition is Apache-2.0; more feature-complete Community features use the Timescale License and cannot be offered as a service. [edition/licence comparison](https://github.com/timescale/docs/blob/latest/about/timescaledb-editions.md) | **DEFER.** Choose only if the project already standardizes on Postgres and needs relational product state beside time series. It is not the simplest replay store. |
| **ClickHouse** | Apache-2.0, very active, columnar real-time analytics at far larger event volumes; strong for long-retention, high-cardinality aggregation. [official repository](https://github.com/ClickHouse/ClickHouse), [time-series fit](https://clickhouse.com/resources/engineering/what-is-time-series-database) | Highest schema/operations/tuning burden here; unnecessary for a small symbol set and one demo. | **DO NOT USE now.** Revisit only when data volume reaches sustained multi-million events/sec or many tenants need concurrent analytics. |

The audit property comes from **content and integrity**, not the database brand. Each normalized observation and firewall decision should have a canonical serialization, content hash, predecessor hash (per symbol or stream), policy/config/model version, and source metadata. Periodically anchor a Merkle root or signed checkpoint outside the process. Retain raw-provider payload hashes even when licensing prevents retention or display of the raw payload itself.

## 5. Anomaly, change-point, and calibration libraries

| Candidate | Exact capability and maturity | License / risks | MarketBridge fit and verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **River** | Online, one-sample-at-a-time anomaly detection, drift detection, statistics, preprocessing and progressive validation. Includes ADWIN, Page-Hinkley and KSWIN; v0.25.0 shipped May 2026; BSD-3-Clause. [official repository](https://github.com/online-ml/river), [drift API](https://riverml.xyz/latest/api/overview/) | Drift alarms are distribution-dependent and can false-trigger around session changes, corporate actions and volatility regime shifts. River itself warns that most problems do not require online learning. | **USE narrowly in shadow mode.** Run ADWIN/Page-Hinkley on residuals such as venue-mark minus robust equity reference, feed age and spread—not raw price level. It may tighten deterministic risk or create an incident annotation; it must never relax a hard safety rule. Calibrate separately by market session. |
| **ruptures** | Offline change-point segmentation with PELT, binary segmentation, dynamic programming and multiple parametric/non-parametric costs; BSD-2-Clause. Latest tagged v1.1.10 focused on packaging/CI maintenance. [official docs](https://centre-borelli.github.io/ruptures-docs/), [releases](https://github.com/deepcharles/ruptures/releases) | Offline/batch by design, and penalty/model choice materially changes breakpoints. | **USE for replay and evaluation, not the live gate.** Segment historical residual/spread/staleness series to label incident boundaries, discover thresholds and show before/after policy performance. |
| **MAPIE** | Scikit-learn-compatible conformal prediction; `TimeSeriesRegressor` supports EnbPI/ACI intervals and updating conformity scores. v1.4.1 shipped June 2026; BSD-3-Clause. [time-series regression API](https://mapie.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api/regression/), [repository metadata](https://github.com/scikit-learn-contrib/MAPIE/blob/master/pyproject.toml) | Coverage is marginal and depends on calibration assumptions; time-series nonstationarity/session shifts can invalidate a naive interval. It also adds model/calibration complexity if the current fallback is deterministic. | **PILOT only** for a clearly labeled estimated/reference interval and abstention threshold. Use rolling, session-aware coverage tests. Do not turn a conformal interval into a claim that a mark is correct, and do not replace deterministic source qualification. |
| **Evidently** | Batch/reporting library with data-quality checks and 20+ drift tests/distances, local HTML/JSON output and dashboards. v0.7.21 shipped March 2026; Apache-2.0. [data-drift preset](https://docs.evidentlyai.com/metrics/preset_data_drift), [package metadata](https://github.com/evidentlyai/evidently/blob/main/pyproject.toml) | Large dependency/UI surface and batch reference-vs-current semantics overlap with MarketBridge's own evaluation dashboard. Its default test selection is generic rather than market-microstructure aware. | **DO NOT ADD for the hackathon.** Borrow the idea of coverage/drift reports, but calculate the small set of domain metrics directly. Reconsider for a post-hackathon model-governance report if multiple learned models exist. |

Recommended live features are interpretable: cross-provider deviation in bps, venue-vs-reference deviation, bid/ask containment, spread, update age, source count, provider count, venue count, clock skew, sequence gaps, session, and transition time since open/close. Every learned score must expose which of these features moved and must be bounded by deterministic policy.

Demo reliability should be product-visible. Include a preflight that verifies each credential/feed entitlement, shows source readiness and session status, then switches to a bundled replay if live feeds are unavailable. The replay must exercise the exact normalizer and engine used live. A judge should be able to disconnect one provider, inject stale/crossed/manipulated evidence, and see bounded degradation rather than a blank dashboard.

## Three differentiated product ideas

### 1. Mark Passport: a verifiable receipt for every decision

Every published `ALLOW`, `DEGRADE`, `ESTIMATE`, or `FREEZE` decision gets a compact passport: normalized witness set, provider/venue independence, event and receive timestamps, exclusions with reason codes, deterministic thresholds, model contribution (if any), policy version, predecessor hash, and signed checkpoint/Merkle proof. The UI answers **“why is this mark safe?”** and **“what changed since the prior mark?”** in one click. This is an evidence product, not another trading chart.

### 2. Oracle Chaos Lab and public safety scorecard

Turn incident replay into an adversarial benchmark: inject a poisoned provider, stale WebSocket, delayed packets, clock skew, crossed quote, sequence gap, overnight liquidity collapse, and 50% external-price shock. Run old and candidate policies deterministically and report time-to-detect, bad-mark exposure in bps-seconds, false-freeze time, coverage, recovery time, and replay parity. Ship named, reproducible scenarios and a shareable scorecard. Competitors can copy a dashboard; a growing corpus of labeled oracle-failure scenarios and policy outcomes is a defensible moat.

### 3. Session-aware Oracle Risk API

Expose a read-only API that converts heterogeneous 24/7 evidence into a bounded operational contract: `safe_reference`, uncertainty band, confidence, maximum allowed mark step, expiry, and action (`allow/degrade/freeze`) with reason codes. Policies explicitly change across regular, extended, overnight ATS and closed/estimated regimes. A HIP-3 deployer or risk engine can consume the contract without trusting MarketBridge to hold signing keys. Over time, calibrate bands from empirical replay coverage and publish provider reliability histories without redistributing raw licensed quotes.

## Evidence limits and stopping point

Provider pricing, entitlements and market-data rights change and can depend on professional status, display/non-display use, derived works, and redistribution. The linked first-party pages are the current public evidence, not legal approval; obtain written confirmation before a public or commercial deployment. “Independent provider” also does not prove independent upstream venues, so MarketBridge must retain venue/source lineage and avoid double-counting composites.

Research stopped after each of the six lanes had at least one high-confidence, actively maintained primary-source candidate and the material contradictions were bounded: free IEX versus full SIP coverage; open-source SDK license versus underlying data rights; live versus offline anomaly methods; broker durability versus audit immutability; and embedded storage simplicity versus server-scale databases.
